A Second Synthetic Trial of Melatonin in Adults with Insomnia
Probe Author, Other Author

Abstract
This simulated report describes a parallel-group trial in adults. It is
a test fixture for the PDF pipeline, not a study.

Methods
Participants were randomised in a 1:1 ratio using a computer-generated
list. Allocation was concealed in sealed opaque envelopes prepared by an
independent statistician without contact to the participants. The trial
was approved by the local ethics committee and conducted in accordance
with the Declaration of Helsinki. Caregivers recorded sleep diaries for
seven consecutive nights before and after the intervention, and the
primary analysis used the mean of the last three nights to reduce
night-to-night variability.
Adverse events were recorded at every visit and classified by severity;
no serious event was attributed to the intervention. Sample size was
planned for a difference of ten minutes with a standard deviation of
fifteen minutes, a two-sided alpha of 0.05 and a power of 0.80. Analyses
used a mixed model with baseline values as a covariate; the values below
are unadjusted group means. Compliance was checked by counting returned
capsules, and a sensitivity analysis excluded participants below eighty
percent of the expected count.

Results
Sixty-two participants were analysed. Sleep onset latency was
24.1 +- 8.4 min in the melatonin group (n = 32) and 31.7 +- 9.9 min in
the placebo group (n = 30).
Sleep efficiency improved slightly in both groups.

Discussion
These numbers are invented for testing and carry no evidential value.
